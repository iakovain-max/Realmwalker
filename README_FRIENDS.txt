REALMWALKER
===========

A grim, open-world fantasy role-playing game in the style of Dwarf Fortress
adventure mode and Caves of Qud, built on Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition rules.
You create a character in a large generated world and do whatever you like:
travel, trade, fight, scheme, survive... and eventually die. The world goes on.

Version 1.0: drawn in pixel art (people dressed in what they really wear,
towns, dungeons, a map on parchment, fights with animation), and playable with
the mouse alone.

For adults (18+): violence, cruelty and grim themes.


WHAT YOU CAN DO
---------------
* Travel a generated world of kingdoms, clans, free cities, tribes and outlaws,
  with 250 years of its own history behind it and politics that keep moving
  while you play: wars, plagues, sieges, new rulers, gangs rising and hanging.
* Make a character (eight short steps, Enter through anything you don't care
  about), then live: walk, camp, cook, hunt, trade, haggle, work for a day's pay.
* Stay alive: hunger, thirst, cold, heat, exhaustion, wounds that bleed and
  fester, sickness, and treatment that matters (bandage, clean, stitch, splint).
* Fight in rank-based D&D 5e combat, with wounds, morale, surrender and prisoners.
* Talk to everyone. They have names, trades, memories and opinions of you.
* Hire companions, pay them, keep them loyal (or don't), and command them in a fight.
* Take on work: bounties, lairs to clear, beasts to hunt, letters to carry,
  heirlooms to find, captives to free, debts to collect, people going missing.
  Press j for your journal. Not every job is what you were told it was.
* Go underground: cellars, crypts, hideouts, caves and barrows, with traps,
  locked doors, secret rooms, chests, and something waiting at the bottom.
  Take a torch. Press s to search.
* Find magic items, learn what they do (or ask a priest), and beware the cursed ones.
* Kill a dragon, if you think you can. The world will remember who did it.
* Die. It is permanent. The world keeps your story and goes on without you, and
  you can begin a new life in the same world (New Character on the title screen).


HOW TO PLAY
-----------
1. Unzip Realmwalker.zip anywhere (for example your Desktop or Documents).
2. Open the Realmwalker folder and double-click Realmwalker.exe.
3. Press F1 in the game for help and all the keys (it lists every one).
4. It saves itself as you play. There is no reload: death is permanent.
5. The mouse does everything: click the map to walk there, click a person to
   talk, right-click anything to see what you can do with it, and every action
   is a button along the bottom (its key is in the corner). The mouse wheel
   zooms the map. No key needs Shift.
6. Esc, then Settings: text size, Graphics (pictures, or the old letters),
   Animations (full, calm, or off), map scales.
7. Saved Worlds on the title screen lists your worlds; Delete moves one to the
   Recycle Bin.

You do not need to install Python or anything else.


1) INSTALL PLAYER2 FOR AI-WRITTEN TEXT (free)
---------------------------------------------
The game uses the free Player2 app to write names, descriptions and dialogue.
  * Download it from https://player2.game and log in.
  * Keep it running while you play.
The small indicator in the bottom-right corner of the game shows
"AI online" when it is connected.

Player2 gives a limited amount of free AI "energy" each day. The game uses it
carefully, and when it runs out the game switches to its built-in text.


2) THE GAME ALSO WORKS WITHOUT PLAYER2
--------------------------------------
Without Player2 (or when its daily energy runs out), the game uses its own
built-in text. Everything still works; the writing is just less varied.


3) WINDOWS MAY WARN YOU
-----------------------
Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC" from an unknown app,
because this game is not from a big publisher. To run it:
  click "More info", then "Run anyway".


WHERE THINGS ARE SAVED
----------------------
Your settings, saved games, screenshots and logs live in:
  %APPDATA%\Realmwalker
(Paste that into the address bar of a File Explorer window.)
If something goes wrong, the file logs\errors.log in that folder helps fix it.
